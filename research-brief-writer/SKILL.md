# Research Brief Writer

## Steps

1. Clarify the topic and scope in one sentence. If the scope is very broad, narrow it to the most useful angle and say what was left out.
2. Gather sources. Prefer primary sources (official data, original studies, primary documents) over commentary. Note the date of each source.
3. Group the findings into the main points that the sources agree on.
4. Identify disagreements: places where sources conflict, where experts hold different views, or where evidence is thin. Record which sources take each side.
5. Write the brief using the structure below.

## Output structure

**Topic and scope** (1 sentence)

**Summary** (3 to 5 sentences)

**What sources agree on** (3 to 6 bullets, each with a source)

**Where experts disagree** (for each disagreement: the positions, who holds them, and the evidence for each)

**Gaps and uncertainty** (what the evidence does not settle)

**Sources** (numbered list: title, author or publisher, date, link)

## Rules

- Every factual claim needs a source. If you cannot source a claim, leave it out or mark it as unverified.
- Present disagreements fairly. Give each position its strongest version.
- Do not state a personal conclusion on contested questions. Lay out the positions and let the reader weigh them.
- Note the date of the most recent source, and say if the topic is fast-changing.
- Keep the brief under 600 words unless the user asks for more.
